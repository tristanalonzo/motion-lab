A black pill that dips under your finger and springs back.

October 2026

thought process

Press fast, release slow. Going down should feel like it's happening under your finger, so it gets 80ms — short enough that you don't watch it, you just feel it. Coming back up is the part you actually look at, so it gets 220ms and a curve that carries it a little past its own size before it settles.

iOS buttons barely move. The dip only goes to 0.96; at 0.9 it stops reading as a press and starts reading as a shrink. The dim and the scale go together — iOS mostly dims, and the scale is what makes it feel like a physical thing. Either one on its own looks flat.

Regular weight on the label, not iOS's semibold, which came out too heavy at this size on a page this quiet.

inspirations

  • Every button in iOS, which dims more than it moves.
  • Apple's own pill buttons — fully round ends, never a rounded rectangle.
  • The feel of a laptop key: fast down, slow up.

what could come next

  • The cursor's hand could squish on the press. Right now it ignores it.
  • Some slack before sliding your finger off cancels the press. Real iOS gives you room; this lets go immediately.
  • A pending state, then an arc to success — the thing a submit button is actually for.
